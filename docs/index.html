Get Compass

Architecture

Two pipelines. One editor. No compromises.

Local and cloud are completely separate AI pipelines that share the same native editor. Each is tuned for what it does best, and neither pretends to be the other.

Local pipeline<100ms

Always on. Always private. No network required.

  • Real FIM completion, intelligent ghost text in <100ms
  • Inline AI popover, cursor-anchored Q&A, on your machine
  • Semantic search, HNSW ANN, 10–50× faster, powered by the Neural Engine
  • Private local chat: ask, bounce ideas, get a second opinion
  • Model: 4B on-device LLM via MLX, accelerated by Apple Silicon
  • No orchestration, no tool loop, by design

Cloud pipelineAgentic

On demand. Full reasoning. Your keys, your choice.

  • Orchestration graph: Planner → Worker → QA → Final
  • Tool loop with stall detection + recovery
  • RAG context injection with evidence fusion ranking
  • 20+ tools, file I/O, terminal, search, project ops, built-in browser
  • Any provider: OpenRouter, Kilo, DeepSeek, Alibaba, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint
  • Opt-in only: nothing leaves your Mac until you say so
4B

Our on-device model is 4 billion parameters, running via MLX on Apple Silicon. Big enough to be genuinely useful, small enough to stay instant and private, and we never ask it to do cloud work.

Most "AI" editors silently ship your source to someone else's servers before they'll autocomplete a single line. Compass never does. Completion, retrieval, and chat run locally; and cloud agentic work happens only when you explicitly reach for it.

Why native wins

Native means something here

Compass is a real macOS binary, compiled in Swift and accelerated by the Metal and Neural Engine frameworks. No Chromium. No Electron event loop burning your CPU. No gigabytes of JavaScript parked in RAM. Just an editor that feels like part of the OS.

~200 MB, AI included

The entire app, with every AI capability switched on, fits in roughly 200 MB. Smaller than most editors manage with AI turned off.

No Electron tax

Tired of an IDE eating 80% of your RAM and slowing to a crawl? No JS loops, no listeners, no unaccelerated UI rendering. Just native performance.

Liquid Glass & deep OS access

Full access to low-level Apple OS features, including the latest Liquid Glass framework, something a web wrapper can never touch.

On-device LLM

Apple MLX running a quantized 4B model. Instant, private, offline. Swap models anytime without a website update.

HNSW - FAISS

Hierarchical Navigable Small World index over FAISS vectors. 10–50× faster than brute-force with ~95–99% recall.

SQLite Index

Symbol and content search over indexed files and chunks, combined with vector retrieval for grounded answers.

Fair questions

Asked and answered, in the open

The questions everyone asks before switching. The answers are the same ones we give on GitHub, HN, and Reddit.

Is the agent as good as Cursor's?

Agentic quality is the model you bring. Compass gives you the orchestration (Planner → Worker → QA with 20+ tools and stall detection), and you choose the brain: OpenRouter, Kilo, DeepSeek, Alibaba, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Bring the best model in the world; we run it at native speed. Our orchestration is open and harness-tested, and the tests are in the repo.

It's not notarized. Is it safe?

MIT licensed, ~40k lines of Swift, CI green, and you can build it from source if you want to verify. The honest reason it's unsigned: Apple charges $99/year for notarization and Compass makes $0. Until sponsors fund it, your Mac will warn you once: right-click → Open, or use Homebrew. We'd rather be transparent than pretend.

One maintainer. Will this die like other projects?

Compass is the editor I use every day to build itself. It's MIT, so even if I vanished tomorrow, it's yours, forever. I'm not planning to vanish: the roadmap is public, the code is open, and the community decides what comes next.

Why macOS-only?

Deliberately. Going deep on Apple Silicon is how the app stays ~200MB with sub-100ms completion and Neural Engine acceleration. There are plenty of editors that work everywhere. There's one native AI IDE for macOS.

What about native Anthropic/OpenAI support?

On the public roadmap. Until it lands, OpenRouter gives you Claude and GPT through a single key, today.

Local models are weak, aren't they?

We never ask local to do cloud's job. Local handles what must be instant and private: completion, Q&A, search, chat. Cloud handles what needs frontier reasoning: agentic work with your key. Right tool for the job, always.

Where are the benchmarks?

Live. Semantic search p95 is 0.46ms on a 2,000-vector index, 2,000 entries add in 158ms, embeddings run at 3–15ms on-device. Full table, methodology, and regression gates on the Benchmarks page, and you can run ./run.sh benchmark yourself. FIM latency with the 4B model is next, and we won't publish it until we've measured it.

I have years of muscle memory in Cursor.

Keyboard is keyboard. Command palette, familiar editing, native macOS feel. Fifteen minutes and you're home. Your Mac gets a gigabyte of RAM back.

Can my team use it?

Yes. One brew line to onboard, your team's keys, usage-based cloud instead of per-seat. Zero egress by default tends to make security teams happy.

How do I install it?

Homebrew with the cask (ships with v0.7). Until then: DMG from GitHub Releases, right-click → Open the first time. That's the whole ritual.
